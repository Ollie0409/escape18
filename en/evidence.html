Evidence

This page says what we know, how we know it and what we do not know yet. In short: the lessons have been made and tested, but not yet taught in a real class. There is no research on their effect yet.

2EVIDENCE

Why these topics

Around your 18th birthday a lot changes at once. You pay your own health insurance premium, apply for student finance, get your first payslip and perhaps your first rental contract. If nobody at home explains it, you often learn it only by making a mistake.

With money and paperwork, things often go wrong in the years after 18, for example with the health insurance premium. That is why pupils practise before they turn 18. We do not say that a 45-minute lesson prevents problems.

3EVIDENCE

How a lesson is built

  • Real kinds of documents, made-up content. Pupils read a payslip, a policy, a letter or a rental contract. The people, companies and amounts in those documents are made up. The rules are real. We do not use the branding or logo of real organisations.
  • Four rooms, one code. Each room gives one digit. Four digits open the lock. Pairs work on one screen.
  • Start and end check. Four questions before and four different questions after, about the same rules. There are two versions (A and B), so a pair never gets the same questions twice.
  • A source with every explanation. Every explanation in the lesson says where the rule comes from. The teacher screen has the full list of sources.
  • Plain language. The Dutch pupil text is checked for reading level (B1). The English editions use plain English and keep key Dutch terms, with a short explanation.
  • Made with AI. The lessons and the English versions were built and checked with the help of AI (Claude). We check rules and amounts against official sources. Do you see a mistake anyway? Email it and we will fix it.

6EVIDENCE

The evaluation plan

As soon as classes teach the lesson, we look at:

  • Use: how many classes, how many pairs started the lesson and how many opened the lock.
  • Start and end check: mean, median and spread per lesson. Also how many pairs already had everything right at the start.
  • Difficulty per question: right first time, hints, attempts and time.
  • Teachers: would you teach the lesson again, what worked and what did not.

What we may and may not say was fixed in advance:

  • We may say: "In N classes, pairs scored on average X out of 4 at the start and Y out of 4 at the end." And: "Z of the K teachers want to teach the lesson again."
  • We may not say: that the lesson caused that gain. There is no comparison group, the check itself can teach something, and pupils work in pairs.
  • We may not use: words such as "effective" or "proven".
  • No conclusions from fewer than about 5 classes at 2 or more schools.
  • A first statement about learning comes only with at least 3 real classes, each with at least 3 pairs that did both checks. Fewer classes? Then we write: "Too few classes for a result about learning."

We also publish disappointing results.

If a school and teacher want it, this can be made stronger later: a class that only does the start check as a comparison, or the same check two to four weeks later with the same class code. We will only build that once a school takes part.

7EVIDENCE

The stop rule

We decided in advance when we stop. If fewer than 3 real classes have taught a lesson by 1 April 2027, we stop approaching schools. We then offer the lessons under the open licence to an organisation that wants to take them over. What is online and what you printed, you can keep using.

The lessons are under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: free to use, share and adapt, not for commercial use, with attribution, and you share your adaptation under the same licence.

8EVIDENCE

Privacy

Pupils make no account and type no name. The teacher chooses Yes or No per class for sharing answers. With No, nothing is sent. Never kept: names, text that pupils type themselves, IP address, kind of device. All data is deleted by 31 January 2027 at the latest; if the project continues, by 31 August 2027 at the latest. See the privacy page.
