How to teach one lesson of Escape 18

A 45-minute lesson about turning 18 in the Netherlands. Pairs solve four rooms with realistic documents. You open the link on the classroom screen. About 15 minutes to prepare. Free, no account.

Ready in 5 minutes

From link to room 1

This is how you start the lesson in class.

  1. 1????

    Open the teacher screen

    Pick your lesson and open the teacher screen. Do this on the computer connected to the classroom screen.

  2. 12???

    Choose Yes or No and start

    Choose Yes or No for sharing answers. Not sure? Choose No: the lesson works fully and nothing is sent. Tap Start the lesson. You get a class code, a link and a QR code.

  3. 123??

    Put the board in projection mode

    The class code, QR code and clock are shown large on the board. Never put the answers on the board. They are on your printed answer sheet.

  4. 1234?

    Pupils join

    Pairs open the short link or scan the QR code, type the class code and tap Start. First comes the start check: four questions, give no hints.

  5. 12345

    Room 1: start the class timer

    Walk around and point to the rule, not to the answer. At the stop time in the lesson plan, tap Stop the escape.

45-minute lesson plan

How a lesson runs

The minutes differ a little per lesson. The exact plan is on the teacher screen of each lesson.

  1. 2 min
    Class start

    Pairs switch on their device. Then the screen closes for a moment. Ask the class one or two open questions about the topic.

  2. 2 min
    Explain and log in

    Put the teacher screen on the board. Pupils open the short link or scan the QR code. Two pupils share one screen.

  3. 2 min
    Start check

    Each pair answers four questions. They do not see the answers yet.

  4. 26 min
    Escape

    Start the class timer. Pairs solve the rooms and write down one digit per room. With all the digits they open the lock. Around minute 28 to 33, tap Stop the escape.

  5. 3 min
    End check

    Different questions about the same rules. Each pair sees what it got right, before and after.

  6. 4 min
    Discussion

    Start with the room that was hardest. The teacher screen has discussion questions and the answers.

  7. 5 min
    Wrap up

    Ask two or three pupils to say their if-then plan out loud. Ask the closing question and fill in the short feedback form.

  8. 1 min
    Spare

    For a login that takes longer, or a discussion that runs on.

What you need

In the classroom

  • A classroom screen or projector for the teacher screen.
  • One device per pair: a laptop or Chromebook. A phone also works, but only if your school allows phones in class.
  • Pen and paper for the digits, and your printed answer sheet.

Tip: put the link in your school platform beforehand, so nobody has to type it.

No laptop per pair?

Two other ways

Whole class. The pupil screen on the classroom screen; pairs write down their answer per question and you tap the class answer. I have run this format myself, from start check to lock. I have not tested it in a class yet.

On paper. Each lesson has a paper version, on the teacher screen. On paper there are no tips after a wrong answer and nothing is sent.

Start and end check

What you see after the lesson

Four multiple-choice questions about the rules in the lesson, before the rooms and after the lock. Each lesson has two forms, A and B, with different questions about the same rules. Each pair gets A or B first at random, and the other form in the end check.

Did you choose Yes for sharing, and did at least 3 pairs take part? Then after the stop you see a summary of the whole class: per room how many pairs finished, the hardest question, the wrong answer chosen most often, and the average of the start and end check. You never see which pair answered what.

The check is short. It shows how many questions the class got right, before and after. It is not a test, it gives no grade, and it is not research into the effect of the lesson.

Privacy in short

You choose Yes or No

Pupils make no account. I keep no names, no IP address and no text that pupils type themselves. If you choose No, nothing is sent from that class and the lesson still works fully. The paper version never sends anything.

Escape 18 does not work on behalf of your school and receives no pupil data from the school: no names, no accounts, no pupil numbers. Answers are stored with only a random class code and a random code per pair. These are pseudonymised data. Under the GDPR they are personal data, and Escape 18 treats them that way. Because Escape 18 is not a processor (verwerker) for the school, a processing agreement (verwerkersovereenkomst) does not fit here.

Dutch words

Words that stay Dutch

The documents in the lessons are Dutch, so some words stay Dutch. You can give pupils this short list before the lesson.

BSN (burgerservicenummer)
your personal number for government, tax and health care.
DigiD
your login for Dutch government websites, for example the tax office.
Zorgverzekering
compulsory health insurance for everyone who lives or works here, at any age. Under 18 you pay no premium; from 18 you pay the monthly premium yourself, plus a yearly excess (eigen risico).
Toeslagen
benefits from the state, for example for health insurance (zorgtoeslag) or rent (huurtoeslag), if your income is low enough.
Belastingdienst
the Dutch tax office.
DUO
the government office for student finance (studiefinanciering) and the student travel product.
BKR
the credit register. It records loans and some credit.
Loonheffingskorting
payroll tax credit. It lowers the tax on your pay. You have it applied by one employer at a time.

Feedback

A few sentences are enough, also if the lesson went badly.

At the end of the lesson the teacher screen shows a one-minute feedback form: would you teach it again, how much did you use, how many pupils, how many minutes, what worked and what did not. It is only sent when you tap Send feedback. Please use no pupil names.
